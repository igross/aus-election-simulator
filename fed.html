Methodology & Assumptions

Uniform swing model: This simulator applies a single national two-party-preferred (TPP) swing uniformly to all 150 federal seats, with seat-level adjustments. Swings are applied in logit-transformed space (matching the full simulation model).

TPP calculation: From primary votes, minor party preferences are distributed using 2028 estimated preference flows: Greens → ALP at 88.19%, One Nation → ALP at 25.5%, Others → ALP at 54.5%. A calibration offset is applied so that the 2025 baseline primary votes produce exactly TPP 55.22%.

Baseline: ALP ~35%, LNP ~32%, GRN ~12%, ONP ~6%, OTH ~15% producing TPP 55.22% ALP from the 2025 federal election.

Logit transform: The model uses a scaled logit transform log(p/(1-p)) × 25 so that 1 unit ≈ 1 percentage point near 50%. This naturally compresses large swings in safe seats.

Seat-level adjustments (from regression model):

  • Sophomore candidate effect: First-term MPs seeking re-election receive a boost: urban +0.72%, regional +1.04% (direction-adjusted).
  • Sophomore party effect: Parties that won a seat for the first time last election receive an additional boost: urban +1.23%, regional +0.86% (direction-adjusted).
  • Previous swing regression: Seats that swung more than their state average last election partially revert: coefficient -0.126 applied to the deviation from state average.
  • Local effects are normalised by subtracting the state average local effect, ensuring they don't shift overall state totals.

Seat types: ALP vs Coalition seats receive the full uniform swing. Crossbench vs Coalition seats also receive the full swing (anti-Coalition swing helps the crossbench incumbent). Crossbench vs ALP seats receive the swing reversed. ALP vs Greens seats receive a dampened swing (30% factor).

One Nation preferential count: In ALP vs Coalition seats, seat-level primary votes are estimated by scaling DemosAU MRP poll estimates proportionally to the national ON slider. A full step-by-step preferential elimination is then simulated from 5 candidates (ALP, Coalition, ON, GRN, OTH) down to 2, eliminating the lowest candidate each round and redistributing their votes. This correctly determines which two parties reach the final 2PP — including cases where the 2nd and 3rd place parties are close.

Preference flows at each elimination:

  • OTH eliminated: ALP 40%, Coalition 30%, ON 20%, GRN 10%
  • GRN eliminated: ALP 82%, Coalition 8%, ON 5%, OTH 5%
  • ON eliminated: ALP onp_alp (configurable), Coalition remainder
  • ALP eliminated (ON vs Coalition): ON alp→ON (configurable, default 35%), Coalition remainder
  • Coalition eliminated (ON vs ALP): ON coal→ON (configurable, default 65%), ALP remainder

When parties not in the base flow have already been eliminated, preferences are normalised proportionally among remaining candidates. The three configurable preferences are set in the Preference Assumptions panel.

Positive margin = ALP-favourable (or incumbent-favourable for crossbench seats). For ON wins, positive margin = ON-favourable.

Slider constraints: Primary votes must sum to 100%. Moving one slider proportionally adjusts all other unlocked sliders. Lock a slider to freeze its value.
