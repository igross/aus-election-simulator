Methodology & Assumptions

Uniform swing model: This simulator applies a single statewide two-party-preferred (2PP) swing uniformly to all 88 Victorian Legislative Assembly seats, with seat-level adjustments and regional swing deviations. Swings are applied in logit-transformed space.

2PP calculation: From primary votes, minor party preferences are distributed using estimated preference flows: Greens → ALP at 81.94%, One Nation → ALP at 25.5% (adjustable), Others → ALP at 49.25%. A calibration offset is applied so that the 2022 baseline primary votes produce exactly 2PP 54.74%.

Baseline: ALP ~37%, Coalition ~34%, GRN ~11%, ON ~4%, OTH ~14% producing 2PP 54.74% ALP from the 2022 Victorian state election.

Logit transform: The model uses a scaled logit transform log(p/(1-p)) × 25 so that 1 unit ≈ 1 percentage point near 50%. This naturally compresses large swings in safe seats.

Regional swing deviations: Seats are grouped into four regional categories (Inner Metro, Outer Metro, Provincial, Rural) with different swing deviations applied to account for differential swing patterns observed historically in Victorian elections.

Seat-level adjustments (from regression model):

  • Retirement effect: Seats where the sitting member is retiring receive a -1.0% penalty (direction-adjusted).
  • Sophomore candidate effect: First-term MPs seeking re-election receive a boost: urban +1.5%, regional +1.0% (direction-adjusted).
  • Sophomore party effect: Parties that won a seat for the first time last election receive an additional boost of +0.5% (direction-adjusted).
  • Previous swing regression: Seats that swung more than their regional average last election partially revert: coefficient -0.126 applied to the deviation from regional average.
  • Transposed federal swing: A scaled factor (0.15) of transposed federal swing is applied to each seat.
  • Local effects are normalised by subtracting the regional average local effect, ensuring they don't shift overall regional totals.

Seat types: ALP vs Coalition seats (83 seats) receive the full uniform swing. GRN vs ALP seats (Brunswick, Melbourne, Richmond) receive a dampened swing (30% factor). The Prahran seat (LNP vs GRN) also receives a dampened swing (30%). South-West Coast (LNP vs IND crossbench) receives the full swing.

5-party preferential model: For ALP vs Coalition seats, seat-level primary votes are estimated using regional baseline estimates for Greens and One Nation, plus national vote shares. A step-by-step preferential elimination from 5 candidates (ALP, Coalition, GRN, ON, OTH) down to 2 is simulated, eliminating the lowest candidate each round and redistributing their votes. If One Nation reaches the final two, the elimination result overrides the TPP-based winner.

Positive margin = ALP-favourable (or incumbent-favourable for crossbench/Green seats).

Slider constraints: Primary votes must sum to 100%. Moving one slider proportionally adjusts all other unlocked sliders. Lock a slider to freeze its value.
